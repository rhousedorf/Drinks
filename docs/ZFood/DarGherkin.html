How to Force a "Dar" to Stay Crisp Since this is a test and you want to use the plants you have, you cannot treat them like a tough-skinned French gherkin. You have to use natural tannins to chemically shield the tender Dar skin from collapsing.Try this exact adjustment to the vinegar-steep method: Clip and Snip Immediately: The absolute second you clip the baby Dar cucumber from the vine, slice off 1/8th of an inch of the blossom end. Because Dar is a softer variety, those natural softening enzymes will ruin it twice as fast.The 2-Hour Ice Shock: Submerge the fresh-cut baby cucumber into a bowl of pure ice water for 2 hours before it touches any vinegar. This forces the soft cells to completely engorge with water and firm up.Add the Tannin Shield: When you put your vinegar, water, salt, and calcium chloride base into the fridge for the 5-day collection cycle, drop one dry Bay Leaf or one pinch of Black Tea leaves directly into the liquid.Why this works: Tannins bind to the proteins and cell walls of tender-skinned cucumbers. It forms a structural "shield" that prevents the high-strength spirit vinegar from melting the cucumber's cellular matrix.Once you accumulate enough over the 5 days, pack them into your Maille jar with the bay leaf tucked inside, and let it sit. If you want to try true commercial cornichons next year, you will need to plant a dedicated French pickling cultivar like Vert de Massy or Parisian Pickling.
